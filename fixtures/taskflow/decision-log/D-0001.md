## Context

NOVA needs a push transport. Three candidates were drawn on the whiteboard on 2026-08-03: poll the inbox, server-sent events, or a WebSocket fanout. The choice is load-bearing well beyond Phase 1 because the Phase 2 offline vault needs a channel too, and a self-hoster should not have to configure two of them.

Input pages: `wiki/sources/notification-architecture-options.md` (the comparison), `wiki/raw/2026/08/2026-08-04-RV-websocket-spike-notes.md` (the measurements), `wiki/sources/selfhost-resource-envelope.md` (the ceiling).

## Answer

**AR-A 2026-08-06 — locked.** WebSocket fanout is the notification transport for NOVA.

Polling is rejected: its cost is inverted (a quiet workspace wastes the most) and the poll interval is a hard floor on staleness, which is the complaint that started the initiative. Server-sent events are rejected: one-directional, so client actions still need separate requests, and the per-origin connection cap penalises the multi-tab power user NOVA is built for.

WebSocket fanout wins on one measured argument and one structural argument. Measured: the spike showed delivery is comfortably fast at self-host scale provided the frame is pre-encoded once per event and the per-socket queue is bounded. Structural: it is bidirectional, so Phase 2 vault sync rides the same connection instead of introducing a second transport.

## Conditions

The decision is locked **with** the four conditions from the source page, all of which are acceptance criteria on WS-2:

1. Pre-encode each payload once per event, not once per subscriber.
2. Bound the per-socket outbound queue; drop and record rather than block the fanout loop.
3. Ship a replay window keyed on the delivery cursor.
4. Document the proxy idle-timeout requirement for self-hosters.

## Impact

- Creates the WebSocket Fanout Service (`wiki/entities/websocket-fanout-service.md`).
- Phase 1 workstreams WS-1 and WS-2 are scoped against this transport.
- Phase 2 sync protocol (WS-5) is a passenger on this channel, not an owner of its own.
- Multi-process deployments are explicitly out of scope until a shared subscriber bus exists.
