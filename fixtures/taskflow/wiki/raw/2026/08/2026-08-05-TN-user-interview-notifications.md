# User interviews — notification fatigue (n=6)

Six thirty-minute interviews with self-hosting teams already running TaskFlow. All quotes are paraphrased from notes; participants are anonymised as P1–P6.

## The recurring theme

Nobody asked for more notification volume. Five of six volunteered, unprompted, that the tools they left behind were abandoned because of notification noise rather than missing features.

> **P2:** "I turned everything off in the old tool within a week. Then I missed the one thing that mattered and turned it all back on. Then I turned it all off again."

> **P4:** "The problem isn't that it tells me things. It's that it tells me things at the same volume whether it's a typo fix or someone reassigning my whole column."

> **P1:** "If it pings me for something I did myself I stop trusting it immediately."

## What people actually want to hear about

Ranked by how often it came up:

1. Something assigned to me (6/6)
2. Someone replied to me directly (6/6)
3. A card I own moved to blocked (4/6)
4. A due date I own changed (3/6)
5. Anything at all on a board I am watching (1/6 — P5, who runs a two-person team)

## Digest versus interrupt

Four of six independently described wanting a once-a-day roll-up for everything except direct mentions. P3 called it "the end-of-day thing" and said they would read it on the train. P6 pushed back — a four-hour cadence, not daily, because their team works across two time zones.

> **P3:** "Interrupt me for a reply. Roll everything else into one thing I read when I choose to read it."

## Self-serving events

Every participant said, in some form, that an event they themselves caused should never come back to them. This is currently the loudest single signal in the research.

## Notes for product

TN's read: the safe starting posture is quiet, with a roll-up as the primary reading surface and direct mentions as the only thing allowed to interrupt. Teams that want more can turn it up; a team that gets shouted at on day one uninstalls. Feeding this into the D-0002 discussion.
